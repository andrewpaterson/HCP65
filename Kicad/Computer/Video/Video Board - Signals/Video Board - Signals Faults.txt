HCP65 Video Board - Signals (V0) - schematic faults
==================================================

Bit numbers below are the local data bus's (/D0-/D7, the sub-boards' D0-D7).
Faults in the sub-boards this sheet uses are in their own Faults files: Video
Clock, Video Counter 12bit, Video Pixel Advance, Video DAC, and in Component
Boards, Clock Hold Standard.


1. The horizontal counter is loaded all through horizontal blank

   B2 (Video Counter 12bit) pin 2 ~{Load} is on /~{Horizontal Blank}, B14's
   ~{Blank} (pin 27).  ~{Load} is the 74LVC161s' synchronous ~PE, which loads
   1.  B14 pin 18 ~{Back_Porch_End}, which is what should load it, is
   unconnected.  The vertical counter is wired the right way: B5 ~{Load} is on
   B7 pin 18 ~{Back_Porch_End}.

   Effect: once horizontal blank starts, H is held at 1 (H3-H10 = 0), so Sync
   Start, Back Porch Start and Back Porch End never match (unless they are
   0).  There is no horizontal sync, and blank never ends: no picture.

   Fix: B2 pin 2 to B14 pin 18.  A line is then 8 x Back Porch End pixel
   clocks.


2. The local data bus is bit reversed at the Device Buffer

   B16 (Device Buffer) pin 34 "D0" is on /D7, pin 36 "D1" on /D6 ... pin 43
   "D7" on /D0.  On the Device Buffer, pin 34 is the edge connector's D0.
   Every other board wires pin 34 to /D0 (VIA, UART, Audio, PS2 ...).

   Effect: the CPU's bit n is the board's bit 7-n.  Every register's bits are
   reversed against the notes (writing Flags bit 0, VSync polarity, sets the
   interrupt enable), and so is the device ROM (IC2): the kernal reads its
   magic and checksum reversed and ignores the board, unless the ROM is burned
   reversed.  What the CPU writes into device RAM (row addresses, palettes)
   reaches the Memory board reversed too, through J4.

   Fix: wire B16 pin "Dn" to /Dn.


3. Horizontal Sync and Vertical Blank are crossed at the delay selector

   B40 (Selector 1-of-4 4bit with Input) lanes: on the sub-board, output Y0
   (J2.32) is lane 0 (inputs A0, B0, C0, E0), Y1 (J2.30) lane 1, Y2 (J2.31)
   lane 2 and Y3 (J2.29) lane 3.  Here lane 0 is Vertical Blank, 1 Vertical
   Sync, 2 Horizontal Blank and 3 Horizontal Sync.  But pin 32 (Y0) is on
   Horizontal Sync _FINAL, the DAC's HSync, and pin 29 (Y3) on Vertical Blank
   _FINAL.  The symbol also has Y1 and Y2 on the wrong pins (it puts Y1 on 31
   and Y2 on 30); those two come out right by accident.

   Effect: the VGA HSync pin carries vertical blank, and the DAC's blank gate
   gets horizontal sync in place of vertical blank.

   Fix: pin 32 to Vertical Blank _FINAL and pin 29 to Horizontal Sync _FINAL
   (or swap lanes 0 and 3 at the inputs); correct the symbol's Y1 and Y2 pin
   numbers.


4. The Pixel Advance counter's count enable floats

   B1 (Video Pixel Advance) pin 32 CEP is unconnected here, and on the
   sub-board it only reaches IC6 (74LVC163) pin 7, with no pull.

   Effect: the 12 step pattern counter counts or holds at random, so the
   pixel address advances at random.

   Fix: B1 pin 32 to 3.3V.


5. The video interrupt can't be cleared

   B8 (Flags & Interrupt) pin 4 ~{Set_Interrupt_on_CLK} is on /~{Vertical
   Blank}, B7's ~{Blank}: a level, low for the whole of vertical blank.  The
   Flags board's interrupt flip-flop samples it on every pixel clock (~Pixel
   CLK_1), and Clear Interrupt ($401) only forces it off while its write
   strobe is high.

   Effect: the interrupt is on for all of vertical blank and comes back one
   pixel clock after it is cleared, so the processor (IRQ is level
   triggered) takes it again and again until vertical blank ends, unless the
   handler turns the interrupt enable off.

   Fix: set the interrupt from a pulse at the start of vertical blank, not
   the level: for instance clock B8 with First Pixel HC and take
   ~{Set_Interrupt_on_CLK} from B7 pin 31 ~{Front_Porch_Start} (low for one
   line, now unconnected), so that it is set once and stays cleared.


6. The Flags & Interrupt's ~{Reset} (B8 pin 32) isn't connected

   As on every board with one: see Component Boards/Flags Enable
   Interrupt/Flags Enable Interrupt Faults.txt.  Reset doesn't turn Enable
   off, and Enable can float.


7. The notes don't match the wiring

   - Pattern registers: the notes say "Horizontal Shift Pattern Low 8 bits"
     ($40A) and "High 4 bits" ($40B).  The wiring is the other way: $40A uses
     D0-D3 (the first 4 ticks of the pattern) and $40B D0-D7 (the other 8),
     as the Pixel Advance sheet's own note (DH7..DH0, DL3..DL0) has it.
   - Signal delay ($417): the notes give 2½, 3, 3½ and 4 clocks.  As wired
     they are 3, 3½, 4 and 4½ clocks after the HC signals: B20 (Video
     Signals Output) is clocked by /Pixel CLK_0 although its symbol's pin 2 is
     named ~{Pixel_CLK}, and the first stage of the Signal Dragger is a whole
     clock.  Clocking B20 on ~{Pixel CLK} would give the notes' figures.
   - $405 bit 3: the note says 1 enables the pixel clock; it stops it (Video
     Clock Faults.txt).
   - "All four comparators will trigger for 8 pixel clock ticks": with fault 1
     fixed, Back Porch End matches for one tick, as H is reloaded.  That is
     fine, but the note no longer holds for it.


8. Each line shows the row table entry for the line before

   The Memory board loads a line's start address from the row table entry
   the vertical counter (Row0-9, J3.1-10) points at, during the front porch
   after the line before it (Last Pixel+1 HC, J3.12).  The vertical counter
   only counts at the start of the next line's active part (B5 is clocked by
   First), so it still holds the line before's number.  The counter loads 1
   at Back Porch End and the first visible line is the one where V is
   Back Porch End, so the visible lines take entries VBPE-1, VBPE, 1, 2 ...
   VFPS-2.  Entry 0 is never used, against the Memory board's "Row 0 Address,
   Row 1 Address" notes.

   Fix: count the vertical counter at the end of each line's active part
   (Last Pixel+1 HC) in place of First, so that it already holds the next
   line's number when the row table is read.  That also gives the vertical
   comparators all of blanking to settle (fault 9).  For entry 0 to be the
   first visible line the counter would also need to start from 0 there, not
   1 (the Video Counter 12bit loads 1 by its wiring, IR0 high), and the
   vertical timing registers would then count lines from 0.  Check the
   vertical blank and sync with the new timing.


9. Timing at high pixel clocks (worst case, from the datasheets)

   - B20 has half a clock to take B14's Blank and Sync: 74LVC74 CP to Q plus
     SD/RD to Q plus 574 setup is about 12.6ns, against 12.5ns at 40MHz and
     7.7ns at 65MHz.
   - The vertical comparators aren't settled when B7 samples them: B7 is
     clocked by First Pixel HC half a clock (plus a 574's delay) after First
     clocks the vertical counter, and the path (161, 1G86, 1G32, F521 ~OE) is
     about 25ns.  Vertical events can come a line late or jitter above about
     16MHz worst case, 34MHz typical.  Clocking the vertical counter at the
     end of the line (Last Pixel+1) would give it the whole of blanking.
   - Advance Pixel reaches the Memory board's counter half a clock after the
     pattern changes (163, PI3B3251, 2G08: about 21ns worst case), so it is
     too late at 65 and 74.25MHz and marginal at 40MHz; ~{Horizontal Blank}
     changes on the very edge it is sampled on.
   - The horizontal comparators are sampled half a clock after H changes
     (about 19ns), so at 40MHz and above an event can come a clock late.
   With the DAC's 50MHz limit (Video DAC Faults.txt) the 65 and 74.25MHz
   clocks can't work; 40MHz is marginal.  1024x768 at 60Hz, the board's
   highest mode, needs 65MHz (Y3, selection 6): so these, and the Memory
   board's (its Faults file, fault 3), have to be fixed for it.


10. Nothing is reset: the board comes up in any state

   None of the write registers has a reset: the timing comparators' 16373s
   (B3, B12, B6, B13, B21, B26), the pattern latch (B1), the palette byte
   (IC3), the clock select (B37's 74LVC574) and the signal delay (B40's
   74VHCT573).  B40's bit 7, which isn't in the notes, is the QS3253s' active
   low enable: until $417 is written with bit 7 low, the sync and blank inputs
   to the DAC float.

   Effect: from power on until the driver has written every register, the
   pixel clock can be any of the eight or stopped, the timing is random (the
   monitor can be sent sync it can't take), the pixel address advances at
   random and the DAC's sync and blank inputs can float.

   Fix: the registers that make the output safe should come up in a known
   state on ~{Reset}: at least the clock select (stopped, or a VGA clock) and
   $417 bit 7 (disabled, with the DAC's blank inputs pulled to blanking), so
   that nothing reaches the monitor until the driver has set the board up.
   A 74LVC574 or 573 has no clear, so that means parts with one (74LVC273),
   or pull-ups or pull-downs held by ~{Reset} through the latch's ~OE.  And
   document bit 7.


Questions (possibly intended)

   - The horizontal registers are in 8 pixel steps.  The modes up to
     1024x768 at 60Hz (1344 clocks: 1024, 24, 136, 160) are all multiples of 8.
     1280x720 at 60Hz (CEA-861, 74.25MHz, Y4), meant for a later HDMI card,
     isn't: 1650 clocks with a 110 front porch, 40 sync and 220 back porch, so
     the nearest is 112/40/216, which a sink may not take.  An HDMI card would
     want the horizontal registers to the pixel, and 74.25MHz is beyond every
     path in fault 9 (the DAC aside, as HDMI is digital).
   - Changing the clock select ($405) while the clock runs switches the
     PI3B3251 live and can put runt pulses into every counter.
   - Enable (B8) gates only the interrupt: the video runs whether or not the
     board is enabled.
   - IC1, the LD1117S50 (SOT-223), drops 12V to 5V for the whole board
     including the DAC: at about 250mA that is about 1.75W.
