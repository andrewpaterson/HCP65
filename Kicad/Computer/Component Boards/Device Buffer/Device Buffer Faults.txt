Device Buffer - schematic faults
================================

From the review of 2026-11-23.

What the board does: it sits between the device bus and one device board's own parts.  IC1 and IC2 (74ABT245, 5V,
DIR high) drive A0-A15 onto the device board while Device ~{CS} is low.  IC3 (74ABT245, OE tied low, DIR high) is
always on and passes ~{RD}, ~{WD}, S (Device CLK), Device ~{CS} and A16 through; its unused inputs are grounded.  IC4
(74ABT245) is the data buffer: A side on the device bus (DD0-DD7), B side on the device board's D0-D7, with DIR =
the buffered ~{RD} and OE = the buffered Device ~{CS}.  The board's A0-A15 and D0-D7 have 10k pull-ups to 3.3V.
Bit order is straight through on every channel.

1. IC4 fights the board's device at the end of every read

   IC4 DIR (pin 1) is the local ~{RD} and its ~{OE} (pin 19) is the local Device ~{CS}.  When ~{RD} rises at the end
   of a read, DIR flips IC4 to A to B (device bus to the board) straight away, while Device ~{CS} is still low.  IC4
   then drives D0-D7 while the board's device (for example the Memory Board's IC5, whose ~{OE} is the same ~{RD}) is
   still turning its outputs off.  There are no series resistors on this board to limit it.  This is the same fault
   as Data and Signal Buffer's fault 1, on the device side of the bus; both buffers turn round together.

   Effect: a short fight on D0-D7 at the end of each device read: supply noise and ground bounce on the device board,
   and on fast parts a corrupted read hold time.

   Fix: take DIR from a signal that doesn't change while IC4 is enabled, and enable IC4 only during a strobe.  For
   example DIR = local ~{WD} (B to A except during a write) and ~{OE} = Device ~{CS} OR (~{RD} AND ~{WD}), one small
   gate on this board.  With that, ~{RD} rising disables IC4 without changing its direction.

2. Possible: ABT high levels onto 3.3V device boards (IC1-IC4)

   The B sides of IC1-IC4 drive the device board's address, data and strobes at ABT levels: about 3.4V typical with
   no maximum given (the 10k pull-ups to 3.3V don't clamp it).  The 5V device boards are fine.  A device board whose
   parts are 3.3V-only (absolute maximum VCC + 0.5V) would be marginal, as in Data and Signal Buffer's fault 5.

   Fix: where a host's devices are 3.3V-only, use 74LVC245s at 3.3V on that side (5V tolerant inputs), or check each
   host's parts are 5V tolerant.
