Selector 12bit - schematic faults
=================================

From the review of 2026-11-23.

What the board does: IC4, IC6 and IC7 (74LVC157, 3.3V, pinout checked) pick A0-A11 (S low) or B0-B11 (S high) onto
Q0-Q11, with a common active-low ~{E}; with ~{E} high the outputs go low, not Hi-Z.  Every host ties ~{E} to ground,
so the outputs are always driven, and none of them shares the Q nets with another driver.  Bit order is straight
through.

1. Possible: one ground pin for the whole board (J1.40)

   J1 has a single GND pin (40) and a single 5V pin (20).  All twelve outputs, three ICs and the regulator return
   through that one pin.

   Effect: ground bounce when many outputs switch at once.  On the Audio Board the selector drives memory addresses
   and the SRAMs' ~{WE} and ~{OE} (B5, Q6, Q7, Q10, Q11), where a glitch can corrupt a write.

   Fix: give the board more ground pins (the hosts' sockets would need them too), or keep this in mind if writes
   through the Audio Board's selectors prove unreliable.
