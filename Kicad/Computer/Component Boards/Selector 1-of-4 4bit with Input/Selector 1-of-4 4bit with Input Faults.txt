Selector 1-of-4 4bit with Input - schematic faults
==================================================

From the review of 2026-11-23.

What the board does: IC1 and IC6 (QS3253, 5V bus switches, pinout checked) pick one of four 4-bit inputs (A, B, C
and E) onto Y0-Y3.  IC3 (74VHCT573, 5V, ~{OE} tied low) is a transparent latch on D0-D7, enabled by Write Select;
Q0 and Q1 are S0 and S1 and Q7 is ~{E} for both switches.  On the Video Board's Signals sheet it picks which
counter's sync and blank signals become the final ones, and Write Select is Device Decode stage B's Y7+W.

1. Possible: the selection and enable are random at power-up (IC3)

   IC3 has no reset, so Q0, Q1 and Q7 come up in any state until software writes the latch.

   Effect: the final sync and blank signals either come from an arbitrary counter or, with Q7 high, float (the
   QS3253 outputs are Hi-Z) until the first write.  The monitor may see garbage or no sync during boot.

   Fix: pull-ups or pull-downs on Y0-Y3 on the host, or a reset on the latch (for example a 74LVC/VHCT273 with ~{MR}
   from ~{Reset}, clocked at the end of the write).

2. Possible: the selection follows the data bus during the write (IC3)

   IC3 is transparent while Write Select is high, so if Write Select is high for the whole write strobe, Q0, Q1 and
   Q7 follow D0, D1 and D7 while the processor's write data is still settling at the start of the strobe.  The
   QS3253 also breaks before it makes when the selection changes.

   Effect: a write that leaves the selection unchanged can still give short glitches on the final sync and blank
   lines, which can upset the monitor's sync for a line or frame.

   Fix: use an edge-triggered register (74VHCT574 or '273) clocked as Write Select ends, so the outputs only change
   once, with settled data.
