Device Decode stage B - schematic faults
========================================

From the review of 2026-11-23.

What the board does: it turns a device's register block into eight read strobes and eight write strobes.
- IC2 (74LVC138) decodes A0-A2, enabled by ~{Device Registers} (~E1), and by ~E2 and E3 from the connector.  The
  host boards wire ~E2 and E3 to A3, ~A3, ~A4 or A4 to pick their block.
- IC4 (74LVC2G34) buffers ~{RD} and ~{WD}.
- Four 74LVC02s make the active-high strobes: Yn+R = NOR(~Yn, ~RD) from IC16 and IC6, and Yn+W = NOR(~Yn, ~WD)
  from IC3 and IC5.

The board is used on the Audio, Blitter (twice), Coprocessor Main, Graphics (twice), PS2, SD Card (twice) and Video
Signals (three times) boards.  As drawn the logic is right, and IC2's and IC4's pinouts are right.

1. All four NOR gates (IC3, IC5, IC6, IC16) have the 74LVC00's pinout, not the 74LVC02's

   The symbol 74LVC02APW,118 in Symbols/Nexperia.kicad_sym numbers its pins like a '00 (1A=1, 1B=2, 1Y=3, 2A=4,
   2B=5, 2Y=6, 3Y=8, 3A=9, 3B=10, 4Y=11, 4A=12, 4B=13).  The real 74LVC02A is 1Y=1, 1A=2, 1B=3, 2Y=4, 2A=5, 2B=6,
   3A=8, 3B=9, 3Y=10, 4A=11, 4B=12, 4Y=13.

   Effect, with the real parts fitted:
   - Pins 10 and 13 of IC16 and IC6 all drive /~{RD}: four outputs against IC4's buffer.  Pins 10 and 13 of IC3 and
     IC5 drive /~{WD} the same way.  The board's read and write strobes are destroyed.
   - Pins 1 and 4 of each chip drive IC2's outputs: ~Y0 and ~Y1 from IC3 and IC16, ~Y4 and ~Y5 from IC5 and IC6.
     Each of those nets has three outputs on it.
   - Pins 3, 6, 8 and 11 of every chip are inputs sitting on the strobe outputs, so all sixteen Yn+R and Yn+W
     strobes to the connector have no driver.  The host board's registers read and latch at random.

   Fix: correct the symbol's pin numbers (it is also used by Device Decode stage A, Processor Full Device, Kernal
   Mode, Device Reset, Instruction Code and MPU NMI) and re-route the four chips.

2. Possible: the write strobes start before the write data is valid

   Yn+W is high for the whole time ~{WD} is low.  ~{WD} falls as PHI2 rises, and the W65C816's write data is only
   valid tMDS later.  A host board that clocks a register on the rising edge of a Yn+W strobe (for example a '574 or
   '273 CP) latches stale data.  A transparent latch whose LE closes on the falling edge (a '573), or an edge clock
   driven from the end of the strobe, is right.

   Check: how each host board uses its Yn+W strobes ("Write Flags", "Write Start", "Write Clear Interrupt",
   "Latch ..." and so on).
