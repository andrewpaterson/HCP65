HCP65 Hold Count - schematic faults
===================================

From the review of the Processor Board's daughter boards, 2026-11-23.

What the board does: IC23 (74LVC163) counts each CLK High Pulse from the Clock Hold board.  IC24 XORs each Count bit
with Latch0-3 (Latch0-2 are H0-H2 on the Processor Board; Latch3 is tied to GND), and IC26 and IC28 reduce that to
L=C (high when equal) and ~{L=C}.  ~{Reset Counter} = ~{L=C} OR ~{CLK High Pulse} goes to IC23's ~MR.  L=C is the
Clock Hold board's ~{Hold High}.

1. The counter is never reset (IC23 ~MR, IC26 gate 4)

   The 74LVC163's ~MR is synchronous: it is sampled one set-up time before CP's rising edge.  ~{Reset Counter} can
   only go low after that edge (the same CLK High Pulse that clocks the counter), and it is high again before the
   next one.

   Effect: ~MR is always high when it is sampled, so the counter free-runs modulo 16.  Every hold lasts until the
   count wraps round to the Latch value, up to about 16 CLKs, whatever H0-H2 say.

   Fix: drive ~MR straight from ~{L=C} (IC26 pin 8) and drop gate 4's CLK High Pulse gating.  Better still, together
   with W65C816 Clock Hold Faults.txt fault 1: clock the counter on CLK's falling edge and reset it synchronously
   while PHI2 is low.  That gives exactly H hold cycles, with L=C settled before each High Pulse.

2. H0-H2 reach Latch0-2 in reverse bit order (on the Processor Board)

   The MPU Memory board's H0 is on its connector pin 44, which is the Processor Board net /H2, so it reaches Latch2.
   Its H2 reaches Latch0.  The main bus's HD0-HD2 come through the Data and Signal Buffer the same way (HD0 to /H2
   to Latch2).  The MPU Memory board treats H0 as the least significant bit.

   Effect: the two sources agree with each other, but counts 1 and 4, and 3 and 6, are exchanged here.  For
   example, MPU Memory's ROM setting of 3 gives 6 hold cycles, and a register access gets 4 in place of 1.

   Fix: decide which end is the least significant bit and check the emulator's assumption.  Then either swap H0 and
   H2 on the Processor Board at the B4 and B8 sockets, or rename the nets to match.

3. Possible: H0-H2 may not be settled in time

   H0-H2 must be stable about one CLK after PHI2 falls: the W65C816's address time (tADS, 30ns), plus the decode on
   MPU Memory or the device, plus about 10ns of compare here.  Near 14MHz that is marginal, and any glitch feeds the
   Clock Hold race (Clock Hold Faults.txt fault 1).

4. Note: this board's presence pin (J1 pin 12) isn't read

   The Processor Board leaves B23 pin 12 unconnected.  See Board Presence Faults.txt, note 3.
