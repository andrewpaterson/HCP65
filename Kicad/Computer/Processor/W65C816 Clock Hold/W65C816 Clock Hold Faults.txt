W65C816 Clock Hold - schematic faults
=====================================

From the review of the Processor Board's daughter boards, 2026-11-23.

What the board does: IC47 (74LVC1GX04) splits CLK IN into CLK (the buffered Y output) and ~{CLK} (the unbuffered X2
output).  IC31 and IC7's inverters make a short pulse on each CLK rising edge (CLK High Pulse) and falling edge (CLK
Low Pulse).  Each pulse is ANDed with ~{Hold High} or ~{Hold Low} (IC1), ORed (IC3), and clocks IC29 (74LVC1G79)
with D = ~{CLK}, which gives PHI2.  A suppressed High Pulse stretches PHI2 high by one CLK.  ~{Hold Low} is tied high
on the Processor Board, so PHI2 low is never stretched.  ~{Hold High} is the Hold Count board's L=C; its polarity is
right (L=C high lets the pulse through, so PHI2 falls on the next CLK rising edge).  PHI2 is a flip-flop output, so it
can't glitch directly.

1. ~{Hold High} changes during the pulse it gates (IC1A into IC29's CP)

   The Hold Count board's counter is clocked by the same CLK High Pulse.  L=C settles 1.5-7.3ns (74LVC163) plus the
   XOR, OR and NOR delays after that edge, about 4.5-21ns.  The pulse itself is about 3-18ns wide (two LVC14 delays
   plus the LVC74's reset-to-Q).  The two windows overlap.

   Effect: when L=C goes high during a pulse, IC29 gets a late clock that may be shorter than its 2.5ns minimum, so
   PHI2 either ends its hold one CLK early or IC29 goes metastable.  When L=C goes low during a pulse, the clock IC29
   has already seen is cut short.

   Fix: make ~{Hold High} settle half a CLK before the pulse it gates: clock the counter from CLK Low Pulse or
   ~{CLK}, or register L=C on CLK's falling edge.  See Hold Count Faults.txt.

2. Possible: the pulse can be shorter than the counter's minimum clock width

   The CLK High Pulse can be about 3ns at the fast corner, while the 74LVC163 needs at least 4.0ns, so the Hold Count
   board can miss counts.

   Fix: more delay in the one-shot's reset path.

3. Possible: ~{CLK} comes from the unbuffered crystal output (IC47 X2)

   X2's output levels are only specified for Y.  Here X2 drives IC31's 2CP, IC29's D, a 10pF capacitor in CN1 and
   the Processor Board's ~{CLK} out to the Data and Signal Buffer, and its skew against CLK isn't matched.

   Fix: make ~{CLK} with an ordinary inverter (a 74LVC1G04) from Y.

4. Possible: ~{Hold High} has no pull-up

   It floats into IC1 pin 1 when the Hold Count board isn't fitted, and PHI2 then behaves at random.

   Fix: 10k to 3.3V.
