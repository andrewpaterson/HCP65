W65C816 Power - schematic faults
================================

From the review of the Processor Board's daughter boards, 2026-11-23.

What the board does: IC1 (LK112M50) makes W65C816V = 5.0V from 12V, enabled by the Processor Board's 5V.  That rail
powers the whole Breakout, including the Breakout's own AP7365 3.3V regulator, so the 74ALVC164245's VCC(B) >= VCC(A)
rule is met.  The unconnected bypass pin is optional.

The W65C816 is overclocked on purpose: it reaches about 40MHz at about 5.3V.  The main processor runs at 25MHz
(Triple MPU Clock Generator) and the coprocessors at 40MHz (Coprocessor Clock), which makes faults 1 and 3 definite.
The user has measured the overclocked W65C816 drawing from under 40mA to 160mA.

1. IC1 can't supply the overclocked W65C816's peak current

   Measured by the user, the overclocked W65C816 draws from under 40mA to 160mA, without getting noticeably warm
   itself.  The heat is in IC1 instead, which drops the rest of the 12V: it is a SOT23-5 with RthJA 255 C/W and an
   output current limit of 150mA (ST datasheet).
   - At 40mA: (12 - 5.25) x 0.04 = 0.27W, a rise of about 70 C.  Warm, but within its limit.
   - At 160mA: about 1.1W, far past what the package can dissipate, and over the 150mA limit.
   On the Processor Board, the Breakout's whole 3.3V side (the data bus, and ~{RD}/~{WD} to about ten boards) is on
   this rail too.

   Effect: at the high end of the range IC1 current-limits or goes into thermal shutdown, so the processor's supply
   dips or drops out.

   Fix: a regulator rated for 200mA or more that can dissipate it (SOT-223 or DPAK with copper), or drop most of the
   12V before it (a switcher from 12V to about 6V, then the LDO).

2. Possible: reset can end before the processor has power

   W65C816V only comes up once both 12V and 5V are present.  If 12V is late, ~{Reset} could release before the
   W65C816 has had a valid VDD and two clocks.  Check this against the reset timing from the mainboard.

3. The supply is 5.0V, not the ~5.3V the overclock needs

   IC1 is an LK112M50, a fixed 5.0V regulator.  The W65C816 needs about 5.3V to reach about 40MHz.

   Effect: at 25-40MHz the processor runs with less margin than intended, or fails.

   Fix: a pin-compatible 5.25V version of the regulator, which the user expects will work (ST's own LK112 range lists
   no 5.25V part, so it comes from another range or maker), bearing fault 1's current and heat in mind.  The parts on
   the W65C816's buses are fine at 5.25V: the 74ALVC164245's and 74LVC4245's 5V side, and the 74VHCT573, are rated
   to 5.5V.
