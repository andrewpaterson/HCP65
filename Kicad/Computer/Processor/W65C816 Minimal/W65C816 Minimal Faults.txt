W65C816 Minimal - schematic faults
==================================

From the review of 2026-11-23.  Only the data transceiver was checked; the rest of the board hasn't been reviewed.

1. The data transceiver is the wrong way round (IC6, 74LVC4245)

   The 74LVC4245's A port runs at VCCA (pin 1, 5V) and its B port at VCCB (pins 23-24, 3.3V).  Here the A port (A0-A7,
   pins 3-10) is the connector's D0-D7 and the B port (B0-B7, pins 21-14) is the W65C816's own data pins (/D0_65 ..
   /D7_65).  The W65C816 (IC2) runs from 5V (VDD on the 5V net).

   Effect:
   - On reads the B port drives the processor's data pins with 3.3V highs, below the W65C816's 4.0V input high at 5V,
     so read data is unreliable.
   - On writes the processor drives 5V into the B port, above its VCCB + 0.5V maximum.
   - The A port drives the connector at 5V.  On the coprocessor boards that is the coprocessor's memory bus, with
     IS61WV5128 3.3V SRAMs that aren't 5V tolerant.
   The W65C816 Breakout has it the right way round: the processor on the 5V side.

   Fix: swap the ports, so the processor's D0-D7 are on A0-A7 (5V) and the connector's on B0-B7 (3.3V), and check DIR:
   with the ports swapped, R/~W high (a read) must drive B to A.

Used on Coprocessor Board - Main (B2, B6) and Coprocessor Board - Additional (B18, B24).  Those boards' own faults
files cover how the sockets are wired (PHI2 into the Latch boards, and ~DE).


Found in the review of 2026-11-23
---------------------------------

What the board does: the W65C816 (IC2) runs at 5V from the host's W65C816 Power board (W65C816V on pins 3 and 23).
IC1 (74LVC2T45, 3.3V A to 5V B, DIR tied high) shifts ~{Reset} and PHI2 up to 5V, PHI2 through R3 (20 ohm).  IC3A
(74LVC139) makes ~{RD} and ~{WD} from PHI2 and R/~W.  IC5 (74VHCT573, 5V) latches the low four bank bits (BA0-BA3,
so 1MB) while Bank Latch is high, out through RN2 (220 ohm), and IC20 (74CBT3244, enabled through IC11 while Bank
Latch is high) bypasses the latch.  RDY is held up by R1; BE, ~{ABORT}, ~{IRQ} and ~{NMI} are tied to 5V.  The
address bus goes out at 5V, which is fine: on both hosts it goes only to 74LVC157s (Selector 12bit) and the
Coprocessor Support's 5V 74F30s and LVC gates.  The pinouts of IC1, IC2 (PLCC-44), IC3, IC5, IC11 and IC20 agree with
their datasheets.  Faults 3 and 4 of W65C816 Breakout Faults.txt (contention at PHI2 rising, and IC20 turning on
inside the read data hold time) apply here too, as this board's bank capture is the same circuit cut to four bits.

2. Not a fault: the W65C816 overdrives the bank latch for a few ns at each bank change (IC5, RN2, IC20)

   While Bank Latch is high, IC5 (74VHCT573) is transparent and IC20 (74CBT3244) is on, so D0-D3_65 -> IC5 D -> Q ->
   RN2 (220 ohm) -> BA0-3 -> IC20 -> D0-D3_65 is a closed, non-inverting loop.
   The design intent (the user's): IC20 lets the bank byte out onto BA as soon as the W65C816 puts it on its data
   pins, and IC5 latches it at the end of Bank Latch (~PHI2, delayed).  For the few ns after a bank change, until
   IC5 follows, the W65C816 drives each changed bit through IC20 (a few ohms) against IC5's old output behind RN2's
   220 ohm, and has to win.  IC5's D inputs are on the W65C816's own pins, so what matters is how far those pins are
   pulled back.

   With an output resistance of about 50 ohm the W65C816's pin loses only about 18% of the swing (about 4.3V for a
   high at 5.3V), well past IC5's 2.0V threshold, and the bits change within a gate delay.  Only by the datasheet's
   minimum output current (0.7mA at VDD - 0.4V, roughly 570 ohm) would it lose, and the user has found the
   W65C816's datasheet very conservative.  Each changed bit still draws a spike of up to about 20mA for those few ns.

   Effect: none.  The user confirms the W65C816 drives fairly hard, so it wins cleanly.

3. R/~W is pulled up to the 3.3V rail (R4)

   R4 (6.8k) goes from R/~W, a 5V W65C816 output, to /3.3V.  BE is tied high, so R/~W is never released and doesn't
   need a pull-up.

   Effect: whenever R/~W is high (most cycles) about 0.25mA flows back into /3.3V.  The AP7365 can't sink current and
   the 3.3V loads here (IC1's VCCA, IC3, IC11, IC6's VCCB) draw only microamps when idle, so the rail can be pulled
   above 3.3V.

   Fix: remove R4 (or take it to 5V).

4. Minor: ~{RD} isn't qualified with VDA or VPA (IC3A)

   ~{RD} is PHI2 AND R/~W alone.  The W65C816 Breakout qualifies its strobes with VPA + VDA; here VDA and VPA are
   unconnected.

   Effect: the internal operation cycles (VDA = VPA = 0) also give a read strobe at whatever address is on the bus.
   On the coprocessor hosts only SRAM is on the bus, so nothing is affected today, but a read-sensitive device on the
   coprocessor's bus would be.

   Fix: none needed for SRAM only; otherwise enable IC3A with VDA OR VPA as the Breakout does.
