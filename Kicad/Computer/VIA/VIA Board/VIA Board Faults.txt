VIA Board - schematic faults
============================

From the socket review of 2026-11-23: every socket's pins were compared with its daughter board.  The rest of the
board hasn't been reviewed.

1. The hold count bits are reversed (B5 VIA Control, pins 54 and 56)

   The board's HD2 (J5.A29) lands on VIA Control's H0, and HD0 (J5.A31) on its H2; HD1 is right.  Every other host
   (Audio, Blitter, Graphics, UART, LCD, PS2, SD Card, Video Signals, Coprocessor Main and both mainboards) puts H0 on
   HD0 and H2 on HD2.

   Effect: VIA Control's ROM count of 3 reaches the bus as 6, and its register counts of 1 and 3 as 4 and 6, so those
   accesses get the wrong number of hold cycles.  RAM's 0 is unaffected.

   Fix: swap B5 pins 54 and 56.

2. The 3.3V flash is driven at 5V (IC3)

   IC3 (SST39VF010, on /3.3V) shares D0-D7, A0-A9 and ~WD with the VIA Breakouts (B1, B4, B6, B7: W65C22S at 5V, which
   drive D0-D7 rail to rail on every VIA read) and the Device Buffer (B2, 74ABT245 at 5V).  The flash's maximum input is
   VDD + 0.5V.

   Effect: over-voltage on IC3's pins, with current through its clamp diodes into the 3.3V rail.

   Fix: 5V-tolerant isolation between IC3 and the 5V bus (for example a 74LVC245 on 3.3V), or run the VIAs at 3.3V.

3. Possible: the VIAs' IRQ pull-ups push current into the 3.3V rail (B1, B4, B6, B7 pins 3 and 26)

   The W65C22S's IRQ output is push-pull at 5V, and RN1 (6.8k) pulls each line up to /3.3V.  Eight VIA outputs high push
   about 2mA into a rail whose only other load is IC3, and the AP7365 regulator can't sink current, so /3.3V can rise.
   With push-pull outputs the pull-ups aren't needed.

4. Possible: a VIA and the Device Buffer can fight on D at the edges of an access (B1, B4, B6, B7, B2)

   Each VIA's R/~W comes from /~WD, so it treats every non-write as a read and drives D whenever it is selected and its
   PHI2 is high.  The Device Buffer's IC4 drives local D (DIR = ~RD) whenever Device ~CS is low and ~RD is high.  If
   ~Select Device is low before ~RD or ~WD go low, or after they go high, the two fight.  The VIAs' PHI2 is a separate
   CLK/2 made on VIA Control, not tied to the strobes, so this depends on bus timing.
