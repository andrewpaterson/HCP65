HCP65 LCD and SNES Board - schematic faults
===========================================

1. The display's E, RS and R/~W buffer is never enabled (LCD Display)

   On the LCD Display board IC11, the 74ABT245 that takes E_Latch, RS_Latch
   and R/~W_Latch from IC13 to the display's E, RS and R/~W, has ~OE (pin 19)
   on 5V.  Its outputs are always off.

   Effect: the display's E, RS and R/~W float.  Nothing written reaches it,
   and it may see accesses of its own.

   Fix: ~OE (IC11 pin 19) to GND.  DIR is already on 5V (A to B).

2. The SNES clock is far too fast for a controller (SNES Controller Clock)

   The SNES Controller Clock counts the device clock (Device CLK, J1 B15, the
   MPU's oscillator: 25MHz): Serial CP is IC1's Q3, the device clock / 16, and
   Serial PL is IC2's TC, high for one device clock in every 256.  At 25MHz
   that's a 40ns latch pulse every 10.24us and a 1.5625MHz clock.  A SNES
   console gives its controllers a 12us latch pulse and about an 83kHz clock.

   Effect: the controllers' shift registers are unlikely to load in 40ns or
   shift at 1.5MHz, so the buttons read as nonsense or as nothing pressed.

   Fix: slow both by 16 or more.  Serial CP from IC2's Q3 (the device clock /
   256, about 98kHz) is close to a console's clock; Serial PL then needs to be
   a pulse at least one of those clocks long (about 10us) once every 16 of
   them, for example decoded from a third 74LVC161 counting them.  The
   registers and the order the bits arrive in stay the same.

Notes (as meant, not faults):
  - Registers: $400-$407 are the display's pins.  Any access to one, read or
    write, latches A0 onto E, A1 onto RS and A2 onto R/~W (IC13, 74LVC16373);
    a write latches its byte for DB0-DB7 too.  $408-$40B read the SNES
    latches: controller 1 (B4) at $408 (~{Read Low}) and $409 (~{Read High}),
    controller 2 (B5) at $40A and $40B.  A4 isn't decoded, so they repeat
    every 16 bytes up to $7FF, and $40C-$40F decode nothing.
  - E falls at the start of the access that drops it, as that access's own
    byte is latched: the display's 10ns data hold time is a race.  Drop E with
    a read (it doesn't change the latched byte), or write the same byte again.
    RS and R/~W change with E as well, so set them with E low first (the
    display wants them 40ns before E rises).
  - The display's datasheet (HCP65/Datasheets/NMTC-S16205DRGHS.pdf, at 4.5V to
    5V) wants E high and low for at least 230ns each and an E cycle of at
    least 500ns: about 6 and 13 cycles of a 25MHz processor, which accesses
    one after another can be closer than.  A driver waits between them.
  - A read with E high is passed through IC20 (74LVC573) during the access, so
    the display's data must be out (up to 120ns after E rises, the
    datasheet's tD) before the access ends.  Raising E with one read and
    reading again with E still high gives it the whole of the first access.
  - The SNES latches: a controller's 16 bits are shifted into IC6 then IC7 on
    the SNES Controller Reader, so IC7 (~{Read High}) holds the first eight it
    sends (B, Y, Select, Start, Up, Down, Left, Right) and IC6 (~{Read Low}) the
    last eight (A, X, L, R and four that are always high).  The readers' D0-D7
    are wired to the board's D7-D0, so the first one sent (B) is D0.  0 is
    pressed; an unplugged controller reads $FF (R1 pulls Serial Data up).
  - ~{Device Enable} (J1 B22) is tied to ~{Device Select} (NT3).  The
    processor board reads the devices' enables (Device Interrupts), and this
    board shows as enabled only while it is being accessed, never when they
    are read.  GND, always enabled like the Small Mainboard's devices 1, 2 and
    7, may be what was meant.
  - ~{Device Interrupt} is tied high (NT1): the board never interrupts.
  - The display's backlight (LEDA, LEDK) isn't connected: the
    NMTC-S16205DRGHS-07 is grey and reflective, with no backlight.  (The
    datasheet is the family's, for the NMTC-S16205DFGHSAY, which has one.)
  - The display's controller is a KS0066 or compatible, with the English
    character set (its datasheet's font table).

The board also uses a standard board with faults of its own, which it has with
it (see its Faults file in Component Boards):
  - Clock Hold Standard: a NOR where an OR is needed, so the clock hold code
    is fought over on every cycle.
