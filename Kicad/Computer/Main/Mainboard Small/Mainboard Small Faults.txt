Mainboard Small - schematic faults
==================================

From the socket review of 2026-11-23: every socket's pins were compared with its daughter board.  The rest of the
board hasn't been reviewed.

1. Memory contention never reaches the processor's NMI (B5, Main Memory Small Enable, pins 18 and 19)

   The board's symbol has ~Contention on pin 18, wired to /~NMI1 (MPU0.B4, pulled up by R1).  On the Main Memory Small
   Enable board pin 18 isn't connected; its ~Contention output (IC4 ~1Q) is on pin 19, which the board leaves N.C.
   Mainboard Full's B10 has it right, on pin 19.

   Effect: a main memory contention is latched and disables main memory, but MPU0 is never told: its ~NMI1 just sits
   pulled up.

   Fix: move /~NMI1 to B5 pin 19, and correct the Main_Memory_Small_Enable symbol.


Found comparing the Processor Board with Mainboard Small, 2026-11-23
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The two boards' slot connector (J5 and MPU0) and device connector (J1 and MPD0) match pin for pin, and every signal
crossing between them has exactly one driver.

2. The processor can't reach main memory: its slot is open while PHI2 is low (B12)

   ~Main Access0 is low for the last 10ns of MPU CLK's high time; the Processor Board's PHI2 is MPU CLK inverted, so
   the slot closes as PHI2 rises and never overlaps the processor's ~RD or ~WD.  See Triple MPU Clock Generator
   Faults.txt, faults 1 and 2 (the processor clock is also 33MHz).

3. ~ABORT is pulled up to 5V (R4)

   R4 (3.3k) pulls /~ABORT to /5V.  On the Processor Board, ~ABORT goes to the W65C816 Breakout's 74ALVC164245 3.3V
   (A port) input, IC1 pin 32, which also has RN4 (4.7k) to 3.3V.  The line idles at about 4.3V, above the A port's
   VCCA + 0.5V maximum, and current flows into the Breakout's 3.3V rail.  Nothing on this board drives ~ABORT.

   Fix: pull R4 to /3.3V (or remove it: the Breakout already pulls ~ABORT up).

4. Minor: ~NMI1 and ~NMI2 are also pulled up to 5V (R1, R2)

   They only reach the Processor Board's MPU NMI 74LVC14 inputs, which are 5V tolerant, so this is harmless; 3.3V would
   match the rest of the slot.  ~NMI2 is driven by IC7 (MAX6816, push-pull at 5V), which is likewise fine for those
   inputs.

5. Possible: the main buses sit at mid-rail two thirds of the time (RN1-RN4)

   AM and DM are biased to /1.6V by 10k networks.  With one processor, only its slot drives them, so for two thirds of
   each cycle every CMOS input on the main bus (the memory boards' SRAMs and buffers) sits at 1.6V and draws
   shoot-through current.  Pull-ups or bus-hold would be better.

Checked and fine on this board:
- MPU0 and MPU1 identity pins are pulled to GND (3.3k), so the processor reads MPU 0.
- ~Reset comes from the Processor Reset Device (IC7, 74LVC14); the Processor Board only receives it.  (Its header
  J2 pin 4 is also on ~Reset: a button there would fight IC7.)
- The device enables for devices 1, 2 and 7 are tied to GND, and the Processor Board pulls up the rest.
- ~Device, Kernal and Native Latch only go to test points here.
- Devices 1 (Main Memory Channel), 2 (Status and Clock Device, split on A4) and 7 (Processor Reset Device) each
  drive HD0-HD2 for their whole select.  Devices 5 and 6 have no board, so an access there leaves HD0-HD2 floating
  (Data and Signal Buffer Faults.txt, fault 2).
- Possible: the device buses (AD, DD, D~RD, D~WD) are driven by the Processor Board's 5V 74ABT245s; their high level
  (about 3.4V) reaches 3.3V parts here, such as the Clock Device's RTC (IC8).  This is marginal against those parts'
  VCC + 0.3-0.5V maximum.
